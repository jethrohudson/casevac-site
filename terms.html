1. Emergencies: call 911 first

CASEVAC is not an emergency service and is not a substitute for calling 911 (or your local emergency number), for emergency medical services, or for the judgment of trained medical professionals.

  • In an emergency, call 911 first. The app’s Call 911 button simply opens your phone’s dialer.
  • The app gives no medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Information such as hospital trauma levels, call-ahead scripts, MIST / 9-line templates, and principal medical profiles is there to help you communicate. It must not replace the decisions of EMS, physicians, dispatchers, or flight crews.
  • The app does not decide where a patient should go. Follow the direction of EMS, medical control, and local protocols.
  • Landing-zone information is not certified. The flight crew always has final authority over whether and where to land.
  • Team SOS alerts your teammates through the app. It is not a replacement for 911 or a radio call. Updates can be delayed or dropped while an app is closed.

2. Accuracy of the data

Hospital, emergency-department, trauma-center, and heliport information comes from public sources: the U.S. Homeland Infrastructure Foundation-Level Data (HIFLD), the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), and OpenStreetMap contributors. Coverage is the United States only.

  • That information can be incomplete, outdated, or wrong. Hospitals close, lose or change trauma designations, divert ambulances, and change phone numbers. Heliports change status. Trauma levels are shown only when a source states them; they are never guessed.
  • Drive times and routes come from Apple Maps and are estimates. Traffic, road closures, and conditions change.
  • Weather comes from Apple Weather and aviationweather.gov and may not reflect conditions where you are. Flight-weather text describes conditions and always defers to dispatch. It never says a flight is safe. Station weather can differ from the landing zone.
  • Candidate landing zones found through OpenStreetMap are unverified outlines on a map, not inspected sites.
  • Field notes, team LZs, team-sourced specialties, and overlays are entered by you or your team. We do not check them.

Always verify critical information, for example by calling the hospital, confirming with dispatch, or inspecting the site, whenever time allows. You use all information in the app at your own risk.
